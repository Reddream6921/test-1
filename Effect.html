Flood impacts explained

When Water Rises, Communities Feel the Effects.

Flooding can change everyday life in a matter of hours. Its effects can reach homes, schools, roads, livelihoods, health, and the environment — and some consequences last long after the water goes down.

The bigger picture

Six Ways Flooding Can Affect a Community

The severity of these effects depends on the depth and speed of the water, how long flooding lasts, and how prepared the community is.

01

Human Life and Safety

Fast-moving or deep floodwater can put people in danger, make travel unsafe, and force families to leave their homes. People may also become separated from family members or need help reaching a safe place.

02

Homes and Personal Property

Water entering buildings can damage walls, floors, furniture, appliances, documents, and other belongings. Families may need temporary accommodation while their homes are cleaned and repaired.

03

Roads and Public Services

Flooding can damage roads, bridges, drainage systems, and public buildings. It may interrupt electricity, transport, communications, schooling, and access to medical care.

04

Health and Clean Water

Floodwater can carry sewage, rubbish, and other contaminants into water sources. This can make clean drinking water harder to access and increase the risk of illness. Damp buildings may also create problems after the flood.

05

Jobs and the Local Economy

Floods can damage crops, livestock areas, shops, and workplaces. When businesses close or transport is disrupted, people may lose income and communities can face high repair and recovery costs.

06

Nature and the Environment

Floodwater can erode soil, spread pollutants, and damage habitats. In some places, however, natural seasonal flooding can also deposit nutrient-rich sediment and support floodplain ecosystems.

How long can the effects last?

Short-Term and Long-Term Effects

Some disruptions happen immediately. Others continue while people and communities recover.

Short-Term Effects

  • Evacuation and temporary displacement
  • Flooded roads and interrupted transport
  • Temporary power, water, or communication outages
  • Damage to homes, shops, and public buildings
  • Temporary closure of schools and workplaces

Long-Term Effects

  • Long periods of home repair or rebuilding
  • Loss of income and difficulties restoring businesses
  • Disruption to children's education
  • Ongoing recovery of soil, water sources, and habitats
  • Changes in how communities plan for future floods
Consider the whole community: Older people, young children, people with disabilities, and people who have limited access to transport or emergency information may face additional challenges. Preparedness plans should take different needs into account.

Knowing the Effects Helps Us Prepare.

Flooding cannot always be prevented, but understanding its possible impacts can help people make informed decisions and reduce risks before an emergency.
